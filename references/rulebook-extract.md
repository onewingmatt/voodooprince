## 1. Identity & materials (p.1)

- Title: *Voodoo Prince* — "Das trickreiche Voodoospiel von Reiner Knizia für 2-5 Personen ab
  8 Jahren" (The cunning voodoo game by Reiner Knizia for 2-5 players aged 8+).
- Designer: Reiner Knizia. Art: Stephanie Böhm. Layout: Leon Schiffer. Editor: Thorsten
  Gimmler. Publisher: Schmidt Spiele GmbH, Lahnstr. 21, D-12055 Berlin.
- **86 cards total:**
  - **80 Voodoo cards**: values **0–15 in 5 different colors** (5 suits × 16 ranks).
  - **5 trump-indicator cards** (one per color).
  - **1 overview card** (player-count reference table).
- Also required to play (not included): a pencil and paper for scorekeeping (p.1–2).

## 2. Object (p.1)

Players try to plan their tricks and take them at the right moment. Each player drops out of
a hand after a set number of own tricks, but needs the *other* players' tricks to score:
when you exit you score all the tricks of all others as points. So it pays to exit as late
as possible and to feed tricks to opponents first — but **do not be last**, because the last
player to exit only gets their own trick count as points. **Optimal: exit second-last.**
Whoever has the most points after 5 rounds wins.

## 3. Setup (p.2) — VERBATIM TABLE

> Verwendete Zahlenwerte je nach Spieleranzahl / Anzahl der Spieler: 2, 3, 4, 5
> Zahlenwerte im Spiel: 0-10, 0-10, 0-12, 0-15
> Karten pro Spieler: 13, 13, 13, 14

| Players | Values in play | Cards per player | Deck in play | 0 beats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0–10 | 13 | 55 | 10 |
| 3 | 0–10 | 13 | 55 | 10 |
| 4 | 0–12 | 13 | 65 | 12 |
| 5 | 0–15 | 14 | 80 | 15 |

- At 2–3 players only ranks 0–10 are used (leave the rest in the box). At 4 players add 11
  and 12. Only at 5 players use everything.
- The oldest player shuffles the **5 trump cards** face-down, draws one and lays it face up —
  that color is **trump for the first hand**.
- Then shuffle all Voodoo cards and deal per the table; **the remaining cards are set aside
  (a blind)** — "Die übrigen Karten kommen zur Seite, sie werden erst für die nächste Runde
  benötigt." (They are only needed for the next round.)

## 4. Trick-taking terms (p.2)

- **Stich (trick):** each player in turn plays one card to the table; the set is a trick.
- **Bedienen (following suit):** you must play a card of the same color as the first card of
  the trick; only if you cannot may you play any other color.
- **Trumpf (trump):** a card of the trump color. All trump cards outrank all other colors
  regardless of value. If exactly one trump is in the trick, its player takes the trick; with
  multiple trumps, the highest trump wins.
- **Einstechen (ruffing):** if you can't follow suit, you may play a trump (ruff) or any
  other color (no ruff).

## 5. Which card wins the trick (p.3)

- The highest card played wins. Generally: highest value **in the led suit**. Cards of other
  suits may only be played when void; their value is meaningless.
- **EXCEPTION:** if at least one trump is in the trick, trumps beat everything, and the
  highest trump played wins.
- **Summary box (verbatim):** "Enthält der Stich Trumpfkarten, gewinnt die höchste
  Trumpfkarte den Stich. Ohne Trumpfkarten gewinnt die höchste Karte der angespielten Farbe
  den Stich."
- **Worked example (p.3, verbatim):** 4-player game, Blue is trump. Marie leads a red 4.
  Lukas plays a red 3. Leon has no red and plays a green 12. Nina has no red and plays a
  blue 2. Because Nina played trump, her blue 2 is the highest card; she takes the trick and
  leads the next.

## 6. Play of a hand (p.3)

- 5 rounds total. The **dealer leads the first trick** of round 1; play proceeds clockwise;
  follow suit; winner of each trick leads the next ("Anschließend spielt der Spieler, der den
  Stich gewonnen hat, die erste Karte für den nächsten Stich aus.").
- Winner keeps tricks face-down, separated, so everyone can always see each player's trick
  count.

## 7. Exit threshold table (p.4) — VERBATIM

> Anzahl der Spieler: 2, 3, 4, 5
> Stichanzahl je Spieler: 7, 4, 3, 3

| Players | Max tricks per player this hand |
|---|---|
| 2 | 7 (hand-ending target, special scoring — §10) |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 3 |
| 5 | 3 |

- When a player reaches their number of tricks, the round ends for them: **the trick counts
  of all other players are added up and scored to them**. They lay their remaining hand cards
  aside face-down and take no further part in the hand. **Their won tricks stay in front of
  them because they still count for the other players' scorings** ("Er lässt seine gewonnenen
  Stiche vor sich liegen, weil sie weiterhin für die Wertungen der Mitspieler zählen.").
- **The player to their LEFT leads the next trick** ("Sein Nachbar zur Linken ist jetzt an
  der Reihe, die erste Karte für den nächsten Stich auszuspielen.").
- **Worked example (p.4, verbatim):** 4-player game; Marie wins her third trick and exits.
  Lukas has 2 tricks, Leon 1, Nina 1. Marie lays her hand aside and scores 2 + 1 + 1 = **4
  points**. Lukas (to Marie's left) leads the next trick.

## 8. End of hand (p.4)

- A hand ends when **only one player remains** in the hand. That player scores **as many
  points as tricks they took themselves**. They become the **start player of the next hand**
  and, after looking at their cards, **choose the trump color** for that hand (of their
  choice).
- "Es ist also gut, auf eine späte Wertung zu warten. Aber wer zu lange wartet, den bestraft
  das Spiel …" (It is good to wait for a late score. But whoever waits too long is punished
  by the game…)

## 9. Special cards (p.5) — three special cards per color

### 9a. Voodoo doll "0"

- If the **0 and the highest card of the same color** (10 / 12 / 15 depending on player
  count) **come together in one trick**, the 0 **beats** that highest card. At 2–3 players
  the 0 beats the 10; at 4 players the 0 beats the 12; at 5 players the 0 beats even the 15.
- Otherwise the 0 is the **weakest card** of its color.
- This applies to normal colors **and** the trump color alike. But even when a 0 of a normal
  color beats that color's highest card, a trump card is still superior and wins the trick if
  present.
- **Worked example (p.5, verbatim):** 4-player game, so 12 is the highest card. If the blue 0
  and the blue 12 are in one trick, the 0 beats the 12. But if Red is trump and a red card is
  also in the trick, the red card wins. If the red 0 and red 12 come together, the red 0 wins
  the trick.

### 9b. Trick-splitting cards "5" and "7"

- If a player **wins a trick with a 5 or a 7**, they must split the trick into **two tricks**.
- The mere *presence* of a 5/7 in a trick is NOT enough — the trick must be **actively won
  with one of these cards**. Only then is the trick split.
- The player thus makes two tricks with one trick ("Der Spieler macht dadurch mit einem Stich
  gleich zwei Stiche.").
- **It can happen that a player ends the hand with one trick MORE than the prescribed
  maximum** ("So kann es vorkommen, dass ein Spieler die Runde mit einem Stich mehr als der
  maximal vorgegebenen Stichanzahl beendet.").
- **Worked example (p.5, verbatim):** 4-player game, Blue is trump. Marie plays a yellow 4,
  Lukas a yellow 2, Leon a yellow 8. Nina has no yellow and plays a **blue 5**. Since Blue is
  trump, Nina wins. Nina already had two tricks; with the blue-5 trick she reaches **4
  tricks**, though in a 4-player game she would normally have been out at 3. The hand ends
  for her now; she scores the tricks the other players currently have.

## 10. 2-player rules (p.6) — VERBATIM

- Only values 0–10 are needed; both players get **13 cards**.
- The hand ends when **one player has 7 tricks**. That player scores the **number of tricks
  their opponent won**. The opponent scores as many points as they were **still short of 7**.
- **Worked example (p.6, verbatim):** Carsten ends the hand with 7 tricks. Ulrich has only 2
  tricks. Carsten gets 2 points, Ulrich 5 points. Had Ulrich had 6 tricks, he would have
  gotten only 1 point and Carsten 6 points.

## 11. Further hands & game end (p.5–6)

- Next hands: the player **left standing last** becomes the start player; they shuffle all
  cards, deal the same count per the table, **look at their hand, then choose any suit as
  trump** (laying the corresponding trump-indicator card face up), then **lead** the first
  trick.
- **Game end:** after **5 hands**, the player with the **most points** wins. **In case of a
  tie there are multiple winners** ("Bei Gleichstand gibt es mehrere Gewinner."). No tiebreak
  rule exists.

## 12. What the rulebook does NOT say

- **First-hand dealer:** the oldest player shuffles/deals (p.2) and the dealer leads (p.3).
  Nothing assigns the deal across later hands except the last-standing rule (§8/§11) — i.e.,
  hand 1's dealer is arbitrary, hands 2+ are dealt by the last-standing player.
- **Trick-count scoring at exit**: the rulebook says "all other players" — it does not
  explicitly carve out already-exited players, but the DE text states explicitly that an
  exiting player's won tricks **remain in front of them because they still count for the
  other players' scorings** (§7), so exited players' frozen counts are included. The
  card-sized rules say the same: "They score 1 point for each trick won so far by all other
  players."
- **2p next-hand dealer:** the 2-player section does NOT say who deals/leads the next hand.
  The port's choice (the player who did NOT reach 7 deals next, mirroring "last standing
  deals") is an **inference**, flagged as such.
- **Drawing the trump card in hand 1** is random (shuffle 5 trump cards, draw 1) — no
  player choice.

## 13. Cross-check vs card-sized English rules (BGG filepage 249702)

The card-sized rules agree on every table and mechanic, and add these clarifications
(verbatim-ish):
- "Setup: 2/3P: Use 0-10; 4P: Use 0-12; 5P: Use all cards. Shuffle the Trump Cards, draw 1,
  this is Trump for the first Hand."
- "Hand: At 2-4/5 players deal 13/14 cards to each player."
- "Scoring: At 3/4/5 players, if the winner has won their 4th/3rd/3rd trick: they discard
  their hand & play no more cards this Hand. They score 1 point for each trick won so far by
  all other players. **The player to their left leads the next trick.**"
- "End of the Hand: When all but one player is out. They score as many points as the number
  of tricks they won. **They deal the next round.** After looking at their hand, they choose
  which colour is Trump."
- "Special Cards: 0: If 0 & the highest card (10/12/15, depending on player count) of the
  same colour are played in the same trick, 0 outranks that card. Otherwise 0 is the weakest
  card of that colour. 5/7: If a player wins a trick using the 5 or 7, then the trick is
  split, it counts as two tricks for Going Out and Scoring."
- "2 Player Scoring, Hand ends when one player reaches 7 tricks: First to 7 tricks scores 1
  points for each trick their opponent won. Opponent scores 1 point for each trick they would
  need to reach 7."

## 14. Voodoo Prince vs Marshmallow Test (for the port's second ruleset)

From huzonfirst's comment on Dale Yu's MT review (Opinionated Gamers 2020-10-04) — the
canonical mechanical difference list:
- VP has **80 cards, ranks 0–15 in five suits**; MT has ranks **1–12** in five suits.
- VP cuts the deck by player count (0–10 / 0–10 / 0–12 / 0–15); MT uses the whole deck at
  every count.
- VP deals **13** cards (2–4p) or **14** (5p); MT deals **12** to everyone.
- MT round 1 has **no trump**; VP round 1 trump is random.
- VP: trump only if void in led suit; MT: once trump is on the table, later players may play
  trump even holding the led suit.
- VP has special cards (0 beats top of suit in same trick; 5/7 split); MT has **none**.
- VP: 5 hands, most points wins, **no tiebreaker**; MT: first to **20 points** ends the game
  immediately.
- MT 5p edge case: players still in when the last card is played score 0 (per Dale Yu's
  review) — this "shutout with no trump-chooser" quirk does not exist in VP's rules.
- **Status in this port:** the marshmallow ruleset's setup (deck ranks 1–12, deal 12, MT
  exit thresholds, first-hand no-trump, race-to-20) is **NOT yet verified against the
  Gamewright MT rulebook**. The port currently runs MT with VP deck ranks (0–15), deal 14,
  legacy exit thresholds, rotating trump, and race-to-20. **This is a documented
  divergence/gap, not a verified implementation.** (`RULES.md` flagged the same.)
